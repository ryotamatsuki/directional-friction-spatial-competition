# Stage 7-LSRS — Relevance / Generality Audit

Date: 2026-09-03
Target: Letters in Spatial and Resource Sciences

## Executive verdict

**GO — SPATIAL RELEVANCE IS SUFFICIENT FOR AN LSRS LETTER; KEEP THE CLAIM NARROW.**

The result has a clear spatial mechanism that can be explained without notation. It does not need a welfare or policy extension to justify a Letter. Its principal limitation is functional-form scope: the reversal is proved for the frozen quadratic-plus-one-sided-linear directional cost, not for a general class of directional costs.

## Mechanism without notation

In an ordinary Hotelling price game, moving firms farther apart tends to soften competition and support higher prices. Under one-sided directional access friction, moving the right-hand firm farther away does something else at the same time: it increases the extra directional burden borne by marginal consumers who reach that firm from the left. The right firm therefore faces two opposing forces. Separation weakens competition, but it also makes the firm less attractive to the marginal consumers whose demand disciplines its price. When directional exposure is sufficiently strong, the second force dominates and the right firm's equilibrium price falls even though the left firm's price rises.

This is a spatial mechanism, not an additive firm-quality effect: incidence depends jointly on consumer position, destination, and direction.

## Credible interpretation classes

The model should use generic interpretation classes rather than claim a specific empirical institution without evidence. Credible conceptual environments include:

- spatial access networks where travelling toward one side entails an extra directional burden;
- one-way or direction-dependent circulation/access environments;
- schedules or layouts in which reaching a destination on one side imposes an additional directional cost;
- product/market spaces where moving toward one product along the horizontal characteristic is more costly than moving in the opposite direction.

These are interpretation classes only. The manuscript should not claim that any particular road network, transit market, retail format, or digital platform is literally described by the primitive unless separate evidence is supplied.

## Essential versus tractability assumptions

### Essential to the stated mechanism

- A one-sided directional exposure term that applies to consumers approaching the right-hand firm from the left.
- The exposure must increase when the right-hand firm is displaced farther to the right for the marginal consumers served by it.
- Price competition must remain active so that the exposure effect operates through the marginal consumer.

### Tractability / normalization assumptions

- left firm normalized to location zero;
- common marginal cost;
- uniform consumer density;
- exactly one unit demanded from one of the two firms;
- covered market with no outside option;
- simultaneous uniform mill pricing.

### Functional-form limitation

The quadratic baseline plus linear one-sided directional term is not proven to be innocuous. The paper does **not** have a robustness theorem showing that the reversal survives arbitrary convex baseline costs or arbitrary directional surcharges. This must be disclosed as scope rather than hidden behind the word 'generality'.

## Welfare audit

No welfare proposition is needed for the paper's research question. Under the covered unit-demand price game, prices are transfers between consumers and firms; adding welfare would shift attention to travel/access costs and would require a different question about the social value of exogenous relocation. Stage 3T also showed that reopening endogenous location does not generate the hoped-for E×H uplift theorem.

**Manuscript contract: no welfare section.**

## Direct qualitative implications

The following implications follow directly from the frozen equilibrium formulas and may be stated as interpretation, not promoted into additional headline propositions:

1. Stronger directional friction makes a negative own-price response to rightward displacement more likely because `dp_R*/dr=(4-2r-theta)/3` falls with `theta`.
2. The rival's positive price response to the same displacement is strengthened by directional friction because `dp_L*/dr=(2r+2+theta)/3` rises with `theta`.
3. The directional friction therefore polarizes the price response to a common spatial-separation change: it amplifies the left firm's positive response while weakening and eventually reversing the right firm's response.

These are theoretical qualitative predictions. They are not empirical findings.

## Fixed-location design

The fact that `r` is not chosen inside the model is not a defect relative to the stated research question. The design intentionally isolates the pricing consequence of exogenous spatial separation. Examples of comparative-static interpretations include relocation caused by zoning, infrastructure/layout changes, lease constraints, or exogenous product-position shifts, but the manuscript need not commit to any specific cause.

The paper must say explicitly:

> We isolate the price effect of spatial separation by holding locations fixed and solving the pricing subgame globally.

It must not claim to characterize firms' optimal locations.

## LSRS scope fit

LSRS explicitly publishes shorter, high-quality social-science papers with a spatial dimension and new theoretical results. The present result directly concerns spatial differentiation, directional access costs, and equilibrium prices, and is therefore within the journal's stated scope.
