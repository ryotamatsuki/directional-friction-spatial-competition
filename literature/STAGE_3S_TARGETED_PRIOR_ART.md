# Stage 3S — Targeted Prior-Art Mini-Kill

Date: 2026-09-03

Scope: candidate-specific mini-kill for the ten post-Stage-3R mechanisms. This is not a Stage-6 certification; where full text was unavailable, the evidentiary limit is stated.

## Strongest papers re-opened / verified

### Nilssen (1997)
Tore Nilssen, “Sequential location when transportation costs are asymmetric,” *Economics Letters* 54(2), 191–201. DOI: 10.1016/S0165-1765(97)00017-7.

Verified from the publisher abstract: three firms locate sequentially when movement in one direction is more costly; the paper discusses the entry pattern and the presence of a first-mover advantage under asymmetry.

**Implication:** kills any contribution whose headline is merely “directional/asymmetric transport changes entry order, sequential location pattern, or mover advantage.”

### Lai (2001)
Fu-Chuan Lai, “Sequential locations in directional markets,” *Regional Science and Urban Economics* 31(5), 535–546. DOI: 10.1016/S0166-0462(00)00067-3.

Verified from the publisher abstract: strategic sequential location with directional and noncooperation constraints; continuous directional markets have no SPNE while discrete spaces admit SPNE.

**Implication:** strong overlap for directionality + sequential location + equilibrium/nonexistence stories.

### Cancian, Bills and Bergstrom (1995)
“Hotelling Location Problems with Directional Constraints: An Application to Television News Scheduling,” *Journal of Industrial Economics* 43(1), 121–124. DOI: 10.2307/2950428.

Verified from the UCSB/eScholarship record: hard directional constraint; no pure-strategy Nash equilibrium.

**Implication:** hard-directional benchmark and equilibrium-existence threat.

### Kharbach (2009); Ebina and Shimizu (2012)
Kharbach, “A Unidirectional Hotelling Model,” *Economics Bulletin* 29(3), 1814–1819. Ebina and Shimizu, “A unidirectional Hotelling model revisited,” *Economics Bulletin* 32(4), 2870–2875.

Verified from Economics Bulletin / RePEc records: Kharbach’s hard one-direction purchasing model generated asymmetric locations; Ebina-Shimizu show that Kharbach’s proposed outcome is not SPNE.

**Implication:** a soft-versus-hard comparison is a benchmark, not a contribution by itself.

### Colombo (2011)
Stefano Colombo, “Spatial price discrimination in the unidirectional Hotelling model with elastic demand,” *Journal of Economics* 102(2), 157–169. DOI: 10.1007/s00712-010-0171-y.

Verified bibliographic identity and unidirectional scope. Full theorem mapping was not available from the accessible abstract-level record.

### Sun (2014)
Chia-Hung Sun, “Spatial Cournot Competition in a Linear City with Directional Delivery Constraints,” *Japanese Economic Review* 65(3), 344–374. DOI: 10.1111/jere.12026.

Verified from Wiley: sequential location and transportation-mode choices under directional delivery constraints produce midpoint agglomeration, maximum differentiation and asymmetric dispersion, with welfare analysis.

**Implication:** kills generic claims that directionality produces richer agglomeration/dispersion regimes.

### Cohen and Heifetz (2024)
Alon Cohen and Aviad Heifetz, “Location, Location, Quality: The Fixed Differentiation Principle,” *Review of Industrial Organization* 65, 705–720. DOI: 10.1007/s11151-024-09989-3.

Verified from the open-access publisher version: location-then-price Hotelling with quadratic transportation costs and vertical quality asymmetry. Higher quality asymmetry moves the better firm inward and the worse firm outward while equilibrium distance remains fixed; welfare may rise.

**Implication:** generic “asymmetry changes locations/prices/welfare” claims are not sufficient; a directional result must be structurally distinct.

### Hamoudi and Moral (2005); Hamoudi and Martín-Bustamante (2011)
Hamoudi and Moral, “Equilibrium existence in the linear model: Concave versus convex transportation costs,” *Papers in Regional Science* 84(2), 201–219. Hamoudi and Martín-Bustamante, “Revisiting price equilibrium existence in the linear-city model of spatial competition,” *Papers in Regional Science* 90(1), 179–197. DOI for the latter: 10.1111/j.1435-5957.2010.00308.x.

The 2011 publisher abstract explicitly states that exact regions of location pairs supporting price equilibrium are calculated for convex linear-quadratic transport costs.

**Implication:** Stage 3R existence-frontier route remains bindingly killed.

### van der Weijde, Verhoef and van den Berg (2012 working paper)
“Hotelling Models with Price-Sensitive Demand and Asymmetric Transport Costs: An Application to Public Transport Scheduling,” Tinbergen Institute Discussion Paper 12-119/VIII.

Full accessible PDF inspected. The paper models two competitors choosing fares and departure times, with linear but asymmetric early/late schedule-delay costs. It analyzes location/fare equilibria, asymmetric schedule-delay functions, Stackelberg structures, welfare and second-best regulation. The conclusion states that asymmetric schedule-delay functions generate asymmetric equilibria and that in Stackelberg versions a first mover can select a position that deters undercutting, giving a second-mover advantage.

**Implication:** this is the strongest additional Stage-3S threat. It directly overlaps A, B, E, H and J at the game-architecture level, although its demand is price-sensitive and its transportation-cost functional form differs from the current quadratic-plus-one-sided-linear candidate.

### Ago (2023)
Takanori Ago, “Utility function and location in the Hotelling game,” *Letters in Spatial and Resource Sciences* 16, 2023.

Publisher/RePEc abstract states that a utility-function extension supports any symmetric location pair between minimum and maximum differentiation; equilibrium price and profits can be non-monotone, and the paper interprets a counterintuitive pricing result as strategic complementarity inherent in Hotelling games.

**Implication:** strategic-complementarity language and non-monotone differentiation/price effects are not automatically new.

### Recent sequential-Hotelling threat
A 2025 AppliedMath paper, “The Sequential Hotelling Game with One Parameterized Location,” studies a sequential location-price Hotelling game and reports that the follower generally outperforms the leader in its investigated parameterized-location settings.

**Evidence limit:** this is a recent adjacent result, not treated as a decisive absorption theorem for the current model.

## Whole-game absorption findings for TOP candidates

### B — Side-switching
No single accessible prior paper was found that exactly reproduces the two-firm game with quadratic symmetric distance cost plus a one-sided linear surcharge, sequential locations and simultaneous mill prices, and then proves a side-order reversal. Therefore this is not classified EXACT PRIOR ART.

However, reproducing the economic headline requires only small steps from well-developed asymmetric/directional sequential-location literature: Nilssen already studies entry patterns and mover advantage under directional asymmetry; Lai studies sequential directional location; Kharbach/Ebina-Shimizu study two-stage unidirectional location-price; van der Weijde et al. combine asymmetric direction-dependent schedule-delay costs, price/location and Stackelberg timing. Thus the result would need a very sharp price-mediated ordering theorem to survive Stage 6.

### E — Directional reversal of differentiation’s price effect
This is the cleanest algebraic local distinction found in Stage 3S. No exact accessible theorem was found stating that an increase in spatial separation raises the left firm’s mill price but lowers the right firm’s mill price beyond a directional-friction threshold in this particular soft one-sided environment.

But this remains a conditional fixed-location price comparative static. Under the workflow, absence of an exact theorem plus a new coefficient sign is not sufficient. The closest game architecture—van der Weijde et al.—already uses asymmetric direction-dependent generalized travel costs and endogenous fares/locations, while broader spatial literature shows strategic-substitute/complement and non-monotone price effects under modified Hotelling primitives.

### H — Exposure versus differentiation trade-off
No exact theorem with the same one-sided quadratic-plus-linear primitive was located. Yet the economic outcome—interior/asymmetric differentiation because standard differentiation incentives are countered by another travel/demand force—is well represented in the broader literature. Without a stronger theorem (ordering reversal, strategic-relation reversal, or welfare ranking unavailable in benchmarks), this is not enough.

No recent working paper was found that creates a clean unoccupied contribution slot for the current primitive. This is an absence-of-evidence statement only and is not itself novelty evidence.

## Mini-kill verdict

No candidate has a sufficiently clean combination of (i) proposition-level distance from the closest papers, (ii) an independently important strategic theorem, and (iii) tractability to justify a Stage-4 commitment.

**Verdict: NO-GO — GENUINE DIRECTIONAL FRICTION ROUTE EXHAUSTED as a standalone theory-paper route.**
