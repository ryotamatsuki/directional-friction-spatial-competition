## 1. Frozen research question

How does downstream spatial price competition change when a third-party transport operator reallocates a scarce fixed service resource across directions in response to passenger demand, while one direction also has exogenous background travel demand?

Secondary approved question: how can a minimum service obligation affect global support and welfare properties of the downstream price equilibrium even when that obligation is slack at equilibrium?

No other research question belongs to the frozen paper.

## 2. Frozen contribution statement

The paper studies downstream Hotelling-Bertrand pricing when access quality is generated by a third-party operator that reallocates a common fixed transit-service resource across directions. Retail demand changes the operator's allocation, so a retail price change affects market share directly and also reallocates service toward one direction and away from the rival direction. Exogenous directional background demand makes this feedback asymmetric.

The approved contribution is narrow:

1. a nonempty open parameter set supports a global pure retail-price Nash equilibrium with one-sided strategic substitutability, `BR_L' < 0 < BR_R'`;
2. a minimum service obligation can be strictly slack at that equilibrium yet support it by truncating only sufficiently extreme off-equilibrium service collapse;
3. the sign-asymmetry disappears in the minimum fixed-frequency, no-background-demand, and retail-unresponsive-frequency benchmarks;
4. the operator microfoundation yields an envelope identity linking the access-cost difference entering shopper choice to the marginal derivative of minimized aggregate waiting cost, producing a substantive private/social share wedge.

The paper does **not** claim that strategic asymmetry, Hotelling network effects, transit demand-frequency feedback, tipping, minimum frequency standards, or generic equilibrium stabilization are new.

## 3. Players, objectives, timing and information

### Players

- Retail destination `L` located at endpoint 0.
- Retail destination `R` located at endpoint 1.
- Unit mass of shoppers uniformly distributed on `[0,1]`.
- A third-party transport operator.
- Exogenous background passengers of mass `M` traveling in the L/urban direction.

### Retail firms

Retailers simultaneously choose prices `p_L` and `p_R` and have common marginal cost `c`.

If L serves shopper share `x`, profits are

\[
\pi_L=(p_L-c)x,
\qquad
\pi_R=(p_R-c)(1-x).
\]

### Transport operator

Conditional on shopper share `x`, directional passenger demands are

\[
D_L=M+x,
\qquad
D_R=1-x.
\]

The operator allocates a fixed total service frequency/fleet `F` to minimize aggregate waiting disutility subject to a minimum service requirement.

### Timing

1. Retailers choose prices simultaneously, anticipating the downstream shopper/operator continuation.
2. Given induced directional demand, the operator allocates the fixed service resource according to its cost-minimizing rule subject to minimum service.
3. Shoppers choose between L and R under fulfilled expectations regarding the operator response.

The frozen equilibrium object is the resulting pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium with the operator/shopper continuation solved consistently. No location stage is present.

## 4. Shopper utility and demand

Let `t>0` denote the quadratic spatial mismatch coefficient and `w>0` the waiting-cost scale.

For a shopper located at `z`, utility net of a common gross value `v` is represented by price, quadratic spatial mismatch, and directional waiting disutility. If L receives share `x`, the marginal shopper satisfies

\[
p_L-p_R+t(2x-1)+A h_q(x,M)=0,
\qquad
A\equiv w/F,
\]

where

\[
q\equiv \underline f/F\in(0,1/2)
\]

is the minimum service share and

\[
s^u(x,M)
=\frac{\sqrt{M+x}}{\sqrt{M+x}+\sqrt{1-x}}
\]

is the unconstrained L service share under canonical waiting disutility `w/f`.

The constrained service share is

\[
s_q(x,M)=\min\{1-q,\max\{q,s^u(x,M)\}\},
\]

so

\[
f_L=Fs_q,
\qquad
f_R=F(1-s_q),
\]

and

\[
h_q(x,M)
=\frac1{s_q(x,M)}-\frac1{1-s_q(x,M)}.
\]

When the floor is slack,

\[
H(x,M)
=\frac{1-M-2x}{\sqrt{(M+x)(1-x)}}
\]

and `h_q=H`.

## 5. Technology and costs

Canonical waiting disutility is

\[
a(f)=w/f.
\]

The operator solves

\[
\min_{f_L,f_R}
 w\left(\frac{M+x}{f_L}+\frac{1-x}{f_R}\right)
\]

subject to

\[
f_L+f_R=F,
\qquad
f_L,f_R\ge\underline f.
\]

When the minimum service constraint is slack, the unique operator solution is the square-root allocation above.

No variable transit operating-cost function beyond the fixed service resource `F` is included. Any fixed cost of supplying `F` is constant across the frozen welfare comparisons.

## 9. Frozen proposition register

### Proposition T1 — Global network-mediated strategic asymmetry

There exists a nonempty open set of primitives for which the hardened model admits a global pure-strategy retail-price Nash equilibrium satisfying

\[
BR_L'<0<BR_R'.
\]

**Proof status: PROVED.**

Support: exact algebraic witness, exact continuation monotonicity at the witness, exact global unilateral-deviation comparisons, strict SOC and reaction-slope inequalities, and continuity/open-neighborhood argument. Deterministic neighborhood audit is supporting verification only.

### Proposition T2 — Slack service obligation and global support

A minimum service obligation can be strictly slack at an equilibrium satisfying T1 while changing only sufficiently extreme off-equilibrium operator allocations and thereby supporting the global price equilibrium.

**Proof status: PROVED for the exact witness and an open neighborhood.**

At the exact witness, the same equilibrium is globally supported while the floor is slack over an intermediate interval approximately

\[
0.324091<q<0.344228.
\]

The numerical decimal endpoints summarize root-isolation results for the witness. They are not promoted to universal closed-form thresholds.

### Proposition T3 — Nested interaction identification

The T1 sign pattern disappears in the minimum nested benchmarks when:

1. frequency is fixed;
2. directional background demand is removed;
3. frequency does not respond to retail shopping demand.

**Proof status: PROVED.**

This is identification evidence, not an independent novelty claim.

### Proposition T4 — Operator-envelope welfare identity

When the service floor is slack, minimized aggregate waiting cost is

\[
J(x)=A\left(\sqrt{M+x}+\sqrt{1-x}\right)^2
\]

and

\[
J'(x)=AH(x,M),
\]

which equals the directional waiting-cost difference entering shopper choice. Hence the shopper indifference condition can be written

\[
p_L-p_R+C'(x)=0,
\]

where `C(x)` is real spatial-plus-waiting cost.

**Proof status: PROVED symbolically.**

### Proposition T5 — Private/social share wedge

On a stable interior equilibrium with the floor slack,

\[
C'(x^{NE})=-(2x^{NE}-1)C''(x^{NE}).
\]

Thus, when `C''>0`, if `x^{NE}>1/2` the decentralized equilibrium locally allocates too little shopper demand to L relative to the real-cost minimizing direction, with the symmetric statement for `x^{NE}<1/2`.

At the exact witness,

\[
x^{NE}=0.575,
\qquad
x^{SB}\approx0.702378,
\]

under the same floor `q=1/3`, and the second-best real-cost improvement is approximately `0.011378`, around 1.22% of decentralized real cost under the normalization.

**Proof status: PROVED for the analytic local wedge; PROVED for the witness-specific second-best comparison.**

## 10. Welfare register

Exact shopper consumer surplus is

\[
CS_S
=v-p_Lx-p_R(1-x)
-\frac{t}{3}\{x^3+(1-x)^3\}
-A\left\{\frac{x}{s_q}+\frac{1-x}{1-s_q}\right\}.
\]

If background passengers have gross value `\bar v`, their surplus is

\[
CS_B=M\bar v-A\frac{M}{s_q}.
\]

Retail producer surplus is

\[
PS=(p_L-c)x+(p_R-c)(1-x).
\]

Prices cancel as transfers, yielding

\[
TW=v+M\bar v-c-C_q(x),
\]

with

\[
C_q(x)=
\frac{t}{3}\{x^3+(1-x)^3\}
+A\left\{\frac{M+x}{s_q(x,M)}+\frac{1-x}{1-s_q(x,M)}\right\}.
\]

The policy-relevant welfare comparison frozen for this paper is the decentralized equilibrium versus the **same-floor second best**. The no-floor utilitarian first-best can shut down one direction at the witness and is retained only as a diagnostic showing that the service floor embodies an exogenous coverage/service-availability objective absent from the utilitarian welfare function.

The paper must not claim to derive the socially optimal coverage floor.

## 11. Approved robustness scope

### R1 — Power waiting technology

Approved alternative technology:

\[
a(f)=w f^{-\rho},\qquad \rho>0.
\]

Conditional on directional demands,

\[
f_i
=F\frac{D_i^{1/(\rho+1)}}{D_L^{1/(\rho+1)}+D_R^{1/(\rho+1)}}
\]

when the floor is slack, and the operator-envelope identity survives.

Strict inequalities at `\rho=1` imply local robustness by continuity.

**Status: PROVED for local structural robustness; NUMERICALLY SUPPORTED ONLY for the reported conservative witness range approximately `0.95<=rho<=1.01`.**

No broader universal `rho` interval may be claimed without reopening verification.

### R2 — Institutional interpretations

Approved settings without changing the mechanism:

- directional fixed-route/shuttle systems using deadheading or short-turning under directional demand imbalance;
- demand-responsive transit or shared-mobility fleets repositioned across zones.

An ordinary single retailer need not be large enough to affect service planning. Preferred interpretation: a large retail destination, commercial cluster, or destination zone.

## 12. Closest-paper distinction frozen for writing

The manuscript must state the distinction narrowly. Strategic asymmetry is known from Tombak (2006). Network/clientele effects in spatial price competition are established by the Hotelling-network literature, including Grilo-Shy-Thisse, Griva-Vettas, and Tolotti-Yepez. Transit demand-frequency vicious cycles are known from Bar-Yosef-Martens-Benenson, and minimum frequency/service constraints are standard transport primitives.

The frozen contribution is the **full strategic network**:

`retail price -> shopping demand -> third-party shared fixed-fleet reallocation -> direction-specific waiting/access cost and rival access -> shopping demand -> retail price best responses`,

with directional background demand breaking symmetry and a minimum service rule that may be slack on path but changes large off-path deviations.

No inspected single predecessor through Stage 6 reproduces this complete game and theorem. The paper must nevertheless be positioned as a transport/spatial-competition theory contribution, not as a new general theorem for arbitrary nonlinear network externalities.
