## 4. Prior-art mini-kill

The most important threats are:

- **Nilssen (1997):** sequential location under asymmetric transportation costs, including entry pattern and first-mover advantage.
- **Lai (2001):** sequential directional markets and SPNE nonexistence in continuous space.
- **Cancian–Bills–Bergstrom (1995):** hard directional constraints and pure-strategy nonexistence.
- **Kharbach (2009); Ebina–Shimizu (2012); Colombo (2011):** unidirectional Hotelling location-price line and corrections/extensions.
- **Sun (2014):** directional delivery constraints generate midpoint agglomeration, maximum differentiation and asymmetric dispersion.
- **Cohen–Heifetz (2024):** asymmetry plus endogenous location-price competition yields the Fixed Differentiation Principle and nontrivial welfare effects.
- **Hamoudi–Moral / Hamoudi–Martín-Bustamante:** nonlinear transportation costs and exact price-equilibrium location regions.
- **van der Weijde–Verhoef–van den Berg:** asymmetric transport/schedule-delay costs combined with endogenous fares, locations, Stackelberg structures, mover advantage and welfare.
- **Ago (2023):** modified Hotelling utility can generate the full range of symmetric differentiation and counterintuitive strategic-complementarity price effects.

No exact single paper was found that reproduces every feature of the current candidate game. That is not sufficient for novelty. The surviving candidate results must still differ economically at the theorem level, and none does so strongly enough.

## 5. TOP 3

### TOP 1 — E: directional reversal of differentiation’s price effect

Local middle-sharing algebra gives

\[
\frac{\partial p_L}{\partial d}>0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial p_R}{\partial d}=\frac{4-2r-\theta}{3}.
\]

Thus greater separation can lower the right firm’s candidate price when \(\theta>4-2r\).

This is the cleanest new algebraic effect found. However, it is only a conditional local price comparative static, while the closest asymmetric-transport models already make price/location effects side-specific. Stage 3/4 explicitly requires more than a monotone/sign comparative static.

**Verdict: no promotion.**

### TOP 2 — H: competition-softening versus directional-exposure trade-off

A local right-follower stationary root is

\[
b_+(a,\theta)=\frac{2a+2}{3}-\frac{\theta}{2}+\frac{1}{6}\sqrt{4(a-2)^2-3\theta^2},
\]

with

\[
\frac{\partial b_+}{\partial\theta}<0.
\]

The economic decomposition is coherent: more separation softens price competition but pushes the right firm deeper into the directionally costly side. Yet interior/asymmetric differentiation generated by an additional travel/demand force is not a sufficiently distinct result relative to the closest literature.

**Verdict: no promotion.**

### TOP 3 — B: side-switching / ordering reversal

A price-mediated switch of the follower from the leader’s right to left would be potentially interesting. But the only supporting evidence is old exploratory numerics that predate the global price-game gate. The prior-art burden is especially high because sequential asymmetric/directional location and mover/entry-pattern results are already well established.

**Verdict: no promotion.**

## 6. Algebraic kill of the strongest proposed principle

The most attractive proposed headline was a change in the strategic relation of locations. For the local right-side stationary root,

\[
\frac{\partial b_+}{\partial a}
=
\frac{2\{a-2+\sqrt{4(a-2)^2-3\theta^2}\}}
{3\sqrt{4(a-2)^2-3\theta^2}}.
\]

The formal zero occurs at

\[
\theta=2-a.
\]

But substituting this value into the root gives

\[
b_+=a.
\]

Therefore the apparent complement/substitute sign switch coincides with collapse of the strict follower-right ordering. It is not a within-branch strategic-relation reversal.

This removes the candidate most capable of changing an established spatial-competition principle.

## 7. Whole-game absorption assessment

The current primitive is not classified as EXACT PRIOR ART. Nevertheless, all economically meaningful candidate routes fall into one of four categories:

1. already explicit in directional/asymmetric sequential-location work;
2. already explicit as agglomeration/dispersion or mover-advantage outcomes;
3. only a new local sign/comparative-static result inside an already crowded asymmetric-transport architecture;
4. mechanically generated by the one-sided generalized-cost term rather than a new strategic feedback.

The workflow does not reward category (3) merely because the exact formula differs.

## 10. Strongest novelty threat

The strongest combined threat is the intersection of **Nilssen (1997)** and **van der Weijde–Verhoef–van den Berg**. The former directly covers asymmetric transportation costs with sequential location and mover/entry implications; the latter combines asymmetric direction-specific schedule-delay costs with endogenous price/location, Stackelberg structures, mover advantage and welfare. Together they sharply reduce the value of another two-firm directional-friction location-price variant unless it changes a deeper strategic principle.

## 11. Strongest mathematical threat

Any remaining B/E/H route requires a full global price continuation because the one-sided term creates kinks and regime changes. Stage 3R already established that local FOCs cannot certify Nash equilibrium. The required mathematics is therefore substantial even before solving sequential locations.

This complexity would be justified only by a clearly stronger novelty target, which Stage 3S did not find.
