Dear Editors of Economics of Transportation,

Please consider the manuscript **“When Retail Demand Moves Transit Supply: Spatial Price Competition with a Fixed Fleet”** for publication in *Economics of Transportation*.

The paper studies a transport-economics mechanism in which downstream retail demand affects the allocation of a fixed third-party transport-service resource. Two spatially differentiated retail destinations compete in prices, while a service planner reallocates a common fixed resource across directions in response to passenger demand. Directional background travel demand makes this feedback asymmetric.

The main result is a constructive global-equilibrium theorem: on a nonempty open set of primitives, the downstream price game has a pure-strategy Nash equilibrium in which one retailer’s price response is downward sloping while the rival’s remains upward sloping. The paper also shows that a minimum service obligation can be slack on the equilibrium path yet support the global equilibrium by restricting extreme off-path service withdrawal. A welfare section connects the same transport allocation problem to real waiting costs and a constrained-efficient benchmark under the same service floor.

The manuscript is positioned narrowly. It does not claim novelty for strategic asymmetry, Hotelling network effects, demand-frequency feedback, minimum service standards, or upstream resource scarcity in isolation. Its contribution is the complete interaction between downstream price competition and a demand-responsive third-party shared transport resource, together with the global-equilibrium and welfare implications of that interaction. This positioning matches the journal’s interest in theoretical transportation economics and in interactions between transportation and other areas of economics, including industrial organization.
