# Stage 6R-LSRS — Actual-Proposition Novelty Re-Kill

Date: 2026-09-03
Target: Letters in Spatial and Resource Sciences
Base theorem: Stage 4R-LSRS strict global price equilibrium and opposite price response.

## Executive verdict

**GO — NARROW LSRS NOVELTY SURVIVES.**

The Stage 4R proposition is not absorbed by the strongest re-opened predecessors. The admissible contribution remains narrow: the same increase in one firm's spatial separation from a fixed rival raises the rival's equilibrium uniform price while lowering the moving, directionally exposed firm's own uniform price on an open parameter region of a strict global price Nash equilibrium.

The paper must not claim novelty for non-monotone prices from differentiation generally, opposite price movements from asymmetry generally, or directional/asymmetric transport costs generally.

## Frozen proposition searched

Under

\[
\tau(x,y;\theta)=(x-y)^2+\theta(y-x)_+,
\]

with firm L fixed at zero and firm R at `r`, Stage 4R establishes a strict global pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium on

\[
r\in\left(\frac{\sqrt{17}-1}{4},1\right),
\qquad
4-2r<\theta<\frac{2r(5r-2)}{2-r},
\]

such that

\[
\partial p_L^*/\partial r>0,
\qquad
\partial p_R^*/\partial r<0.
\]

## Re-opened closest literature

### Ago (2023), *Utility function and location in the Hotelling game*

Ago slightly expands consumer utility in a duopolistic Hotelling game and obtains any symmetric location pair between minimum and maximum differentiation as equilibrium. The equilibrium location is monotone in the introduced parameter, while equilibrium price and profit are U-shaped. Hence Ago contains a broader endogenous-location contribution and a counterintuitive price result.

What Ago endogenizes that this paper does not: firms' locations.

What this paper contains that Ago does not: a theorem that a unilateral increase in one firm's spatial separation from a fixed rival can lower the moving firm's own equilibrium mill price while raising the rival's price because the moving firm becomes more exposed to a one-sided directional access burden.

The present paper is narrower than Ago, but it is not an immediate corollary of Ago's utility perturbation. Its mechanism depends on destination direction and consumer location jointly, and it is established on a globally audited price subgame.

Classification: `LSRS QUALITY BENCHMARK / RELATED HOTELLING RESULT — DOES NOT ABSORB`.

### van der Weijde, Verhoef & van den Berg

This is the strongest architectural predecessor: asymmetric transport/schedule-delay costs, uniformly distributed consumers, prices/fares, and locations/departure times. It confirms that asymmetric transport costs can generate asymmetric spatial-price outcomes. The relevant separation logic retains the standard competition-softening interpretation rather than the frozen own-price reversal from the same one-firm displacement.

Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE — DOES NOT ABSORB EXACT THEOREM`.

### Mérel & Sexton (2010)

They characterize weak-duopoly Hotelling equilibria and establish that equilibrium price can fall as the transportation-cost parameter rises. This kills any broad claim that more differentiation can lower price.

Their comparative static changes a transport-cost/differentiation parameter, not one firm's physical location while holding its rival fixed, and does not establish the rival-up / own-down response to the same displacement.

Classification: `KILLS BROAD PERVERSE-PRICE CLAIM — DOES NOT ABSORB FROZEN LOCATION DERIVATIVE`.

### Cohen & Heifetz (2024)

Their quadratic Hotelling model with vertical quality asymmetry shows that greater quality asymmetry can raise the better firm's price and lower the worse firm's price. This kills any broad claim that an asymmetry can move two prices in opposite directions.

The changed primitive is quality asymmetry. Their headline location result is the Fixed Differentiation Principle; the frozen theorem here concerns the price response to increasing one firm's horizontal separation under directional access friction.

Classification: `KILLS BROAD OPPOSITE-PRICE CLAIM — DOES NOT ABSORB`.

### Chakravorty & Sappington (2026)

Consumers can pay to alter a default horizontal characteristic. Default-switching costs can reduce a product's appeal while also committing the seller to compete less vigorously for distant consumers. This is the closest recent mechanism-level threat because it explicitly features an appeal-versus-competition trade-off.

However, the comparative-static object is a switching/default cost, not a one-firm location displacement. The reviewed result does not establish the frozen rival-up / own-down location derivative.

Classification: `STRONG MECHANISM OVERLAP — DOES NOT ABSORB`.

### Kharbach (2009); Ebina & Shimizu (2012)

Kharbach studies a hard unidirectional constraint and a two-stage location-price game. Ebina and Shimizu show Kharbach's claimed outcome is not a subgame-perfect equilibrium. These papers make it impossible to claim novelty for unidirectional Hotelling or for the mere interaction of directionality, location, and mill pricing.

They do not establish the frozen soft-friction uniform-price derivative result.

Classification: `CLOSE DIRECTIONAL PRIOR ART — DOES NOT ABSORB`.

### Nilssen (1997)

Nilssen studies sequential location with directionally asymmetric transportation costs, entry pattern, and first-mover advantage. It absorbs generic claims about asymmetric transport changing spatial location incentives, but not the frozen simultaneous uniform-price comparative static.

Classification: `COMPONENT OVERLAP — DOES NOT ABSORB PRICE THEOREM`.

## Whole-game/result absorption verdict

No re-opened predecessor reproduces all five economically relevant elements at once:

1. covered unit-demand Hotelling duopoly with uniform mill prices;
2. quadratic baseline distance cost plus a soft one-sided directional term;
3. left location fixed and right location varied continuously;
4. strict global pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium on an open region;
5. opposite price responses to the same one-firm increase in separation.

This is sufficient for a narrow Letter contribution; it is not sufficient for a broad claim of a new general differentiation principle.

## Why this is not merely another utility-function perturbation

The contribution is not that a different utility formula changes an equilibrium. The relevant economic object is a directional exposure term whose incidence varies jointly with consumer position and the destination firm's location. Increasing `r` simultaneously weakens price competition and increases the access burden borne by marginal consumers buying from R. The result identifies an open region where the latter channel reverses R's usual competition-softening price response while L's response remains positive.

The paper must nevertheless acknowledge functional-form scope: no theorem is claimed for arbitrary directional-cost functions.

## LSRS strength relative to Ago (2023)

Ago is broader and stronger as a location-theory paper: it endogenizes locations and produces several equilibrium results. The present paper is narrower, but LSRS Letters do not require every contribution to match the breadth of Ago. The present result has three compensating strengths appropriate for a Letter: exact global-equilibrium verification, a sharp opposite-sign comparative static, and an explicit open parameter region with a standard Hotelling benchmark.

Verdict on LSRS floor: `SUFFICIENTLY PLAUSIBLE TO PROCEED`, subject to Stage 7.5 journal-fit gate.

## Defensible LSRS contribution paragraph

Prior work shows that transportation asymmetries affect spatial competition, that Hotelling prices can respond non-monotonically to differentiation, and that other forms of asymmetry can move two firms' prices in opposite directions. This paper isolates a different comparative static. With a soft one-sided directional access cost, moving the directionally exposed firm farther from a fixed rival can lower its own equilibrium mill price while raising the rival's price. The mechanism is a conflict between the usual competition-softening effect of separation and the moving firm's increased exposure to directional access costs. The result holds on an open parameter region of a strict global price Nash equilibrium and disappears in the standard quadratic-Hotelling benchmark.

## Strongest remaining novelty threat

The strongest remaining threat is a paper or appendix in the asymmetric-transport/location-price literature that derives the same one-firm location derivative but does not advertise it in title/abstract. The targeted re-kill found no such theorem or immediate corollary. The correct evidentiary language remains `APPARENTLY OPEN`, not an absolute first-in-literature claim.
