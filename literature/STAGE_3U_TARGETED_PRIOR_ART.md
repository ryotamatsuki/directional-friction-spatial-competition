# Stage 3U — Targeted Prior-Art Map

Date: 2026-09-04

## Purpose

This mini-audit tests whether transport-network directionality combined with a new strategic variable can escape the killed Stage 3R/3S routes.

## Closest families

### Endogenous transportation-cost / accessibility investment

- Hou, Wu & Zhou (2013), *The competition of investments for endogenous transportation costs in a spatial model*, Economic Modelling 31, 574–577.
- Bacchiega, Randon & Zirulia (2012), *Strategic accessibility competition*, Research in Economics 66, 195–212.

Implication: firm investment that simply lowers its own transport/access cost is not new.

### Transit frequency as an endogenous service variable

- Williams & Abdulaal (1993), public transport competition in fares and frequencies.
- Zubieta (1998), network equilibrium with competitive bus-service frequency.
- Wang & Yang (2005), deregulated bus-market competition in price and frequency.
- Zhang, Lindsey & Yang (2018), monopoly/public transit fare-frequency choice and Mohring effects.

Implication: endogenous transit frequency is known in transport economics, but these papers do not make retail firms' downstream price/location choices feed into a shared directional frequency allocation rule.

### Asymmetric transport / scheduling Hotelling

- Nilssen (1997), sequential location with asymmetric transportation costs.
- van der Weijde, Verhoef & van den Berg (2014), asymmetric distance costs with endogenous fares and schedule locations.

Implication: Stage 3U cannot claim novelty from asymmetric travel costs or endogenous timing/location alone.

### Network effects / congestion in Hotelling

- Ahlin & Ahlin (2013), congestion/negative network externalities in Hotelling.
- Tolotti & Yepez (2020), firm-specific network effects with location, price and demand.
- Di Cintio (2007), imitation/crowding effects.

Implication: if the preferred mechanism reduces completely to an ordinary firm-specific network externality, it must be killed.

### Retail access / shuttle / transport infrastructure

- Wang (2020), *Retail competition using free shopping shuttle bus strategies*, Managerial and Decision Economics 41, 1010–1019.
- Fujigaki et al. (2012), commercial-facility cooperation with route buses versus shopping shuttles.
- Kono & Nozoe (2007), transport improvement, store location and cost-benefit analysis.
- Kishi, Kono & Nozoe (2015), central/outlying retail locations under radial/ring-road improvements.
- Nilsson & Smirnov (2016), transportation infrastructure and competing-retailer co-location.

Implication: private shuttle provision, generic transport improvement and retail-location responses are already established objects.

### Infrastructure spillovers / accessibility coordination

- Investment in public infrastructure with spillovers and tax competition between contiguous regions (Regional Science and Urban Economics, 2009).
- Wan, Basso & Zhang (2016), strategic accessibility investment under port competition and inter-regional coordination.
- Matsushima & Mizuno (2018), strategic infrastructure investment with access spillovers.

Implication: generic spillover investment is not enough; any shared-route candidate must exploit one-way route topology or another structural feature.

### Online/offline and trip-chain alternatives

- Colombo & Hou (2021), location-price competition with an online retailer.
- Hu et al. (2014), online/offline demand and logistics costs in a Hotelling model.
- Gokan, Thisse & Zhu (2026 discussion paper), shopping versus shipping.
- Raith (1996), spatial retail markets with commuting consumers.
- de Palma, Dunkerley & Proost (2010), trip chaining and market competition.
- Wrede (2015), endogenous consumer locations in continuous-logit Hotelling.

Implication: delivery bypass, commuting/trip chaining and endogenous consumer location are crowded routes.

## Preferred-candidate novelty hypothesis

No exact paper was located in the searches through 2026-09-04 that combines all of:

1. downstream retail Hotelling price/location competition;
2. a third-party transport operator with a fixed directional service resource;
3. service allocation responding endogenously to retail-generated directional passenger demand;
4. exogenous background commuter demand creating the direction asymmetry; and
5. retail firms anticipating the service-reallocation feedback.

This is only an `APPARENT GAP`, not a novelty finding.

## Mandatory kill comparison for Stage 4

The preferred route survives only if a headline result cannot be reproduced by either:

- a reduced-form firm-specific network-externality Hotelling model; or
- an exogenous asymmetric-access/quality wedge.

The model must produce a full-game interaction generated by the transport operator's fixed-fleet reallocation response.
