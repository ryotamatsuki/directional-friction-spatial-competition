## Canonical model

Consumers are uniform on `[0,1]`, each purchases exactly one unit from one of two firms. Firm L is fixed at `0`, firm R at `r in (0,1)`. Both have marginal cost `c` and set uniform mill prices simultaneously. Directional access/travel disutility is

\[
\tau(x,y;\theta)=(x-y)^2+\theta(y-x)_+,\qquad \theta\ge0.
\]

The covered market is a primitive with no outside option. Accordingly, the common gross valuation can be normalized away and no lower bound on `v` is needed. Introducing an outside option would be a substantive model change and is not authorized.

## Allocation and single crossing

With `q=p_R-p_L`, the utility difference is continuous and strictly decreasing:

\[
\Delta(x)=
\begin{cases}
q+r^2+\theta r-(2r+\theta)x,&x\le r,\\
q+r^2-2rx,&x\ge r.
\end{cases}
\]

Thus every allocation generated by a unilateral price deviation is represented by a cutoff. The inverse cutoff-price relation is continuous at `x=r`, and `x=0,1` include zero demand and full-market capture.

## Equilibrium candidate

Let `h=2r+theta`, `C=r^2+theta r`. The middle-sharing candidate is

\[
p_L^*-c=\frac{C+h}{3},\qquad
p_R^*-c=\frac{2h-C}{3},\qquad
x^*=\frac{C+h}{3h}.
\]

For `r>1/2`, `x^*<r` because

\[
3rh-(C+h)=r(5r-2)+\theta(2r-1)>0.
\]

## Global deviation audit

### Firm R

Within `[0,r]`, deviation profit is strictly concave and uniquely maximized at `x^*`. Because `x^*<r`, the derivative is already negative by the time the cutoff reaches `r`. At the kink the derivative changes by

\[
\theta(r-1)<0
\]

for `r<1`, so it falls further. The outer-regime profit is strictly concave. Hence no cross-kink or full-market-capture deviation beats `x^*`.

### Firm L

Within `[0,r]`, deviation profit is strictly concave and uniquely maximized at `x^*`. For `x\ge r`, the derivative at the kink is

\[
-\frac{10r^2+r\theta-4r-2\theta}{3}.
\]

Thus

\[
10r^2+r\theta-4r-2\theta>0
\]

is a strict sufficient condition under which outer-regime profit is decreasing from the kink onward. Since profit is continuous across the kink and already decreasing from `x^*` to `r`, no cross-regime, zero-demand, or market-capture deviation is profitable.

Therefore the candidate is a **strict global pure-strategy Nash equilibrium** for

\[
r>1/2,\qquad
\theta<\frac{2r(5r-2)}{2-r}.
\]

## Uniqueness claim

The gate supports the statement that, at the displayed equilibrium pair, each firm's equilibrium price is its unique global best response to the rival's equilibrium price. It does **not** establish that no other Nash equilibrium exists in any other price regime. The manuscript must not claim global equilibrium uniqueness unless separately proved.

## Main comparative-static proposition

\[
\frac{\partial p_L^*}{\partial r}=\frac{2r+2+\theta}{3}>0,
\]

\[
\frac{\partial p_R^*}{\partial r}=\frac{4-2r-\theta}{3}.
\]

The price-reversal condition `theta>4-2r` overlaps the strict global-equilibrium sufficient region iff

\[
2r^2+r-2>0.
\]

Therefore, with

\[
\bar r=\frac{\sqrt{17}-1}{4},
\]

for

\[
r\in(\bar r,1),\qquad
4-2r<\theta<\frac{2r(5r-2)}{2-r},
\]

the strict global price equilibrium satisfies

\[
\boxed{\partial p_L^*/\partial r>0,\qquad \partial p_R^*/\partial r<0.}
\]

The interval is open and nonempty.
