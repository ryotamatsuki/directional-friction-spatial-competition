## Preferred mechanism

**Demand-responsive directional transit-frequency allocation with a fixed fleet.**

Two endpoint retailers compete in prices. If the left retailer serves share `x`, urban/left directional transit demand is `M+x` and rural/right directional demand is `1-x`. A transit operator allocates total frequency `F` to minimize waiting cost:

\[
\min_{f_L,f_R>0}
w\left(\frac{M+x}{f_L}+\frac{1-x}{f_R}\right)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
f_L+f_R=F.
\]

The operator response is

\[
f_L=F\frac{\sqrt{M+x}}{\sqrt{M+x}+\sqrt{1-x}},
\qquad
f_R=F\frac{\sqrt{1-x}}{\sqrt{M+x}+\sqrt{1-x}}.
\]

This creates the feedback

`retail price -> shopping demand -> direction-specific frequency reallocation -> waiting/access cost -> retail demand`.

Because total frequency is fixed, demand captured by one direction also withdraws service from the other.

## Key algebraic preflight

With endpoint retailers and quadratic spatial cost `t`, the shopper fixed point is

\[
G(x)=p_L-p_R+t(2x-1)+\frac{w}{F}H(x,M)=0,
\]

where

\[
H(x,M)=\frac{1-M-2x}{\sqrt{(M+x)(1-x)}}.
\]

Exact symbolic verification gives

\[
H_x=-\frac{(M+1)^2}{2[(M+x)(1-x)]^{3/2}}<0.
\]

Hence demand-responsive frequency allocation amplifies retail price sensitivity relative to fixed frequencies.

At `M=0`, `x=1/2`, the local symmetric markup is

\[
p-c=t-\frac{2w}{F}.
\]

A marginal increase in urban-bound background demand gives

\[
\left.\frac{dx^*}{dM}\right|_{M=0}
=\frac{w}{3(Ft-2w)}>0,
\]

\[
\left.\frac{d(p_L^*-p_R^*)}{dM}\right|_{M=0}
=\frac{4w}{3F}>0.
\]

## Strongest theorem target

**Directional Strategic Asymmetry in Retail Prices.**

Let `g=G_x` and `g'=G_xx`. Local price best-response slopes are

\[
\frac{dBR_L}{dp_R}=\frac{g+xg'}{2g+xg'},
\qquad
\frac{dBR_R}{dp_L}=\frac{g-(1-x)g'}{2g-(1-x)g'}.
\]

The full architecture admits a local-equilibrium point with opposite signs. For

\[
t=F=1,
\quad M=\frac15,
\quad x=\frac{11}{20},
\quad w=\frac{27\sqrt{15}}{308},
\]

symbolic verification yields

\[
g=\frac{58}{77},
\qquad
g'=-\frac{128}{77},
\]

both own-price SOC terms are positive and

\[
\frac{dBR_L}{dp_R}=-\frac{31}{114}<0,
\qquad
\frac{dBR_R}{dp_L}=\frac{289}{434}>0.
\]

Thus one retailer can treat its rival price as a strategic substitute while the other still treats its rival price as a strategic complement.

## Nested benchmarks

The candidate effect disappears in the minimum nested games:

- **B0 fixed equal frequencies:** standard quadratic Hotelling; both price BR slopes are `1/2`.
- **B1 demand-responsive frequencies but `M=0`:** symmetry gives `G_xx=0` at the symmetric equilibrium; both slopes are `1/2`.
- **B2 `M>0` but frequency does not respond to retail shopping demand:** directional access shifts demand but does not create the endogenous curvature channel; both slopes remain `1/2` in the quadratic benchmark.
- **Full model:** `M>0` plus demand-responsive fixed-fleet reallocation can make the two reaction slopes have opposite signs.
