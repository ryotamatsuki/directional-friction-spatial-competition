## 2. Mandatory zero-cost publication screen

### Elsevier journals

Elsevier distinguishes subscription publication, funded by readers/subscribers, from open-access publication funded by an APC. The journals below are offered as subscription journals and support an optional OA route. The project therefore treats standard subscription publication as the zero-mandatory-APC route.

Elsevier also states that any journal-specific submission fee is clearly flagged in the journal Guide for Authors and during submission. Current public journal materials inspected for the selected Elsevier candidates did not surface a mandatory submission-fee requirement. Because the user requires zero fees, Stage 13 must re-check the live submission screen before final submission; if a fee is presented, do not pay and move to the next journal.

### Journal of Transport Economics and Policy

The official 2026 contributor guidelines list electronic PDF submission and manuscript requirements but do not list a submission fee or mandatory publication/page charge. They do state that author alterations after the proof stage may be charged; this is avoidable and is not a mandatory publication charge. Because Liverpool University Press acquired the journal from 1 January 2026, the fee status should nevertheless be reconfirmed immediately before submission.

## 4. Primary target — Economics of Transportation

### Current scope match

The current official description states that the journal publishes important contributions to transportation economics, research on interactions between transportation and other economic activities, and cross-fertilization with fields including industrial organization. It explicitly welcomes both theoretical and applied papers.

This is unusually close to the actual surviving contribution: a downstream retail-pricing game whose strategic interaction is changed by a third-party transportation-service allocation layer.

### Recent-paper fit

Recent papers show that the journal continues to publish analytically driven transport-economics work on pricing, capacity, spatially explicit shared vehicles, empty trips, market competition and welfare. Relevant examples inspected include:

- Ming Hsin Lin (2025), *Airport congestion pricing and capacity: Cost recovery with per-flight or per-passenger charges*;
- Dario Farren, Ricardo Giesen and Luis Ignacio Rizzi (2024), *The economics of empty trips*;
- Roman Zakharenko (2023), *Pricing shared vehicles*.

These papers indicate an audience comfortable with analytical transportation economics, pricing, capacity/resource constraints and welfare, although some combine theory with data or richer institutional structure.

### Why it beats the alternatives

1. The transport × IO interaction is part of the stated scope, not a peripheral application.
2. Pure theory is explicitly admissible.
3. The paper's operator-envelope welfare analysis, service constraint and fixed-resource interpretation are economically meaningful to this audience.
4. Compared with TR-B, the paper does not need to pretend that its contribution is a general methodological advance.
5. Compared with RTE, the paper need not add an empirical application merely to justify the theory.
6. Compared with JTEP, there is no hard 35-page/100-word-abstract constraint surfaced as a decisive submission issue in the materials reviewed.

### Desk/referee risk

Subjective assessment: **moderate-to-high but defensible**.

Most likely desk/referee objections:

- “The local game is a nonlinear network effect in transport clothing.”
- “The service planner is institution-specific.”
- “The main theorem gives existence of an open set rather than a complete parameter map.”
- “Why should large destination retail demand materially move public transport supply?”

The Stage-11 manuscript already answers these as far as the frozen theory permits. Stage 13 should improve salience, not add new primitives.

## 5. Realistic fallback — Journal of Transport Economics and Policy

### Fit

The journal defines itself around transport economics and its interface with policy and covers pricing, competition, service quality, economic regulation and all transport modes. It has a long history of analytical transport-pricing work and in 2026 continues to publish theory-heavy airport congestion-pricing/capacity research.

### Current submission constraints

Official guidelines currently specify:

- PDF submission;
- maximum 35 pages, double spaced;
- 12-point Times New Roman;
- one-inch/2.5 cm margins;
- abstract no more than 100 words;
- one combined submission file;
- Word file required after acceptance;
- extended appendices can be placed on an author website;
- proof-stage author alterations may incur a charge.

These are presentation constraints, not theory blockers. A JTEP fallback version should shorten the main text and move computational proof detail to an online appendix/repository.

### Fee status

The 2026 official submission guidelines do not state a submission fee or mandatory publication/page charge. Given the 2026 transfer to Liverpool University Press, Stage 13/actual submission must reconfirm this immediately before use. If any mandatory fee is introduced, JTEP is automatically removed from the ladder.

## 6. Safety net — Research in Transportation Economics

### Fit

The journal's current scope is explicitly economic: applications of economic theory and/or applied economic methodologies to transportation questions, including regulation and planning.

Recent papers include analytical/game-theoretic work such as:

- *Multi-product airport competition and optimal airport charges* (2024);
- *Investigating the market share convergence and welfare potentials of asymmetric train access charges for the commercial passenger rail services* (2024);
- *Booking versus search-based parking strategy: A game-theoretic methodology* (2024).

This demonstrates clear tolerance for strategic pricing/competition models. The current paper should be competitive on technical rigor, but the outlet's policy/industry-decision emphasis makes a sharper institutional motivation desirable.

### Fee status

The journal is a subscription/hybrid Elsevier title. The project will use the standard subscription route. No current public material inspected surfaced a mandatory submission fee. The same zero-fee submission-screen gate applies.

## 7. Stretch comparison — Transportation Research Part B

TR-B's current scope explicitly includes mathematical transport models, pricing/investment and traveler behavior. Recent papers solve strategically richer resource-sharing and pricing games, including joint resource exchange/pricing and organizational competition in rail/air systems.

The present paper is mathematically valid but its headline theorem is intentionally narrow: an exact witness plus strict inequalities establishes a nonempty open set, not a complete global parameter classification or new general solution method. Under the frozen theory, the expected desk/referee risk at TR-B is therefore excessive.

**Decision:** do not spend the first submission on TR-B. Reconsider only if the manuscript later acquires a genuinely broader theorem through formal theory-change control; Stage 12 does not authorize such a change.

## 8. Rejected journals

### Transportation Research Part A: Policy and Practice

Current official scope requires a clear policy concern or practice interest and states that papers must be based on solid research and good-quality data. The frozen paper is a pure theoretical mechanism paper with no empirical application. Fitting Part A would require substantive new evidence or policy design, which is prohibited at Stage 12.

### Transport Policy

Current scope strongly emphasizes analysis/evaluation of transport policies and strategies and explicit policy relevance. The frozen paper has an exogenous service floor but does not optimize regulation or provide an empirical policy evaluation. Repositioning it as a policy paper would overstate the model.
