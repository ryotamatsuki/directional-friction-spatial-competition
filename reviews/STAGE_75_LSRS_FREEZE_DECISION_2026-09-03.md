# Stage 7.5 — LSRS Manuscript Investment Decision

Date: 2026-09-03
Target journal: Letters in Spatial and Resource Sciences

## Executive verdict

**GO TO FULL PAPER / LSRS LETTER.**

The project is too narrow for a broad theory-journal claim but is sufficiently clear, correct, spatial, and proposition-level novel to justify manuscript investment as an LSRS Letter. No additional theory should be developed before drafting.

## Mechanism card

**Phenomenon:** spatial separation ordinarily softens price competition.

**Friction:** consumers approaching the right-hand firm from the left incur an additional one-sided directional access cost.

**Strategic response:** moving the right-hand firm farther away weakens competition but simultaneously increases its exposure to marginal consumers' directional burden.

**Equilibrium effect:** on an open strict-global-NE region, the rival's equilibrium price rises while the moving firm's own equilibrium price falls.

**Welfare effect:** not part of the manuscript claim.

**Empirical implication:** if otherwise comparable relocations increase directional exposure, the moving side's price response can be weaker or negative while the rival's remains positive. This is a theoretical prediction, not an empirical finding.

## 1. More than a functional-form curiosity?

**Qualified yes for LSRS; no claim of generality.**

The theorem is generated by an interpretable conflict between competition softening and directional exposure, not by an uninterpreted coefficient sign. It is also established globally rather than from a local FOC. However, no robustness theorem is available for arbitrary transport-cost functions, so the manuscript must state the functional-form scope explicitly.

For LSRS, which explicitly publishes shorter papers with a spatial dimension and new theoretical results, this is sufficient to justify a Letter. It would not justify a claim to a general differentiation principle.

## 2. Can the mechanism be explained without notation?

Yes. Greater distance usually relaxes price competition, but if moving farther simultaneously makes the moving firm harder to reach in one direction, its price incentive can weaken enough to reverse. The rival does not bear the same directional exposure and retains the usual positive price response.

## 3. Spatial interpretation

Credible. The primitive is genuinely spatial/directional because the extra cost depends on both consumer position and whether the destination lies to the consumer's right. It is not a firm-specific additive quality penalty.

The paper should use generic access/circulation interpretations and avoid asserting a specific institutional application without evidence.

## 4. Distinction from Ago (2023)

Ago is broader as a location-theory contribution: it endogenizes location and shows how a utility expansion spans symmetric location equilibria between minimum and maximum differentiation, with non-monotone prices/profits.

This paper instead isolates a globally solved fixed-location price subgame and establishes an opposite-sign response of the two firms' prices to the same one-firm spatial displacement under one-sided directional exposure.

The present paper is narrower than Ago but not redundant with it.

## 5. Distinction from Mérel–Sexton and Cohen–Heifetz

Mérel–Sexton makes broad perverse price responses to differentiation prior art. Cohen–Heifetz makes opposite price responses to an asymmetry parameter prior art. The contribution here is the same one-firm increase in horizontal separation producing rival-up / own-down responses because the moving firm becomes more directionally exposed.

## 6. Is one clean theorem enough for LSRS?

Yes, conditionally on concise execution. LSRS is explicitly a Letters journal for shorter high-quality spatial papers presenting new theoretical or empirical results. The manuscript therefore does not need a second theorem merely to mimic a full-length theory paper.

The theorem must carry enough proof and interpretation to be self-contained, and the literature boundary must be unusually precise because the result is narrow.

## 7. Critical referee attack: why vary r if location is not endogenous?

### Attack

> Why should we care about changing `r` if firms do not choose locations in the model?

### Approved response

The paper's question is not which locations firms optimally choose. It asks how equilibrium price competition responds to a change in spatial separation when access costs are direction-dependent. Holding locations fixed isolates the price subgame and prevents the price mechanism from being conflated with location incentives. The comparative static is therefore appropriate for exogenous relocation, spatial-layout changes, or product-position shifts. The paper does not claim to characterize optimal locations.

This defense is stronger than reopening Stage 3T. The endogenous-location uplift was separately tested and failed to generate a distinct E×H theorem; it should remain outside the manuscript.

## 8. Essential and tractability assumptions

Essential: one-sided exposure that rises with the moving firm's separation for relevant marginal consumers.

Tractability/scope: uniform density, quadratic baseline distance cost, linear directional surcharge, fixed left location normalization, common marginal cost, covered unit-demand market, simultaneous mill prices.

The linear directional surcharge and quadratic baseline are not proven innocuous. They are model scope, not a general theorem.
