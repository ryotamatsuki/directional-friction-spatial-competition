## Institutional and literature check

Minimum-frequency/headway requirements are standard in transit planning and regulation, often imposed jointly with fleet constraints. The added primitive therefore has an independent service-standard rationale.

However, the following are already known and must not be claimed as contributions:

- minimum public-transit service frequency;
- Mohring demand-frequency feedback;
- vicious/virtuous transit-demand cycles and multiple equilibria;
- strategic asymmetry as a general game-theory concept.

Bar-Yosef, Martens & Benenson (2013) is an especially important new Stage-6 threat because it directly models self-reinforcing bus frequency-demand cycles and multiple equilibria. The current paper must distinguish itself through downstream spatial price competition and the direction-specific fixed-fleet reallocation theorem, not through transit tipping itself.

## New artefact risks

1. **Reduced-form absorption:** after solving the operator, the model produces a nonlinear market-share externality. A referee may argue that the transport institution merely implements a known reduced-form network effect.
2. **Floor-regularization criticism:** although institutionally standard, the minimum floor might be viewed as a technical regularizer. The defense is that it is slack at equilibrium and does not generate the headline sign pattern.
3. **Transportation-literature absorption:** transit economics already studies endogenous frequency, regulation, minimum service, and vicious cycles. Novelty must reside in the interaction with downstream Hotelling-Bertrand pricing.
