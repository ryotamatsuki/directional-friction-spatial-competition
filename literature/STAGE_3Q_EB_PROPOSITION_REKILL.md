## Frozen proposition under review

This gate reviews only the Stage-3P result. No broader claim is admissible.

Fix the left firm at `l=0`, the right firm at `r in (0,1)`, and let firms set uniform mill prices simultaneously under

\[
\tau(x,y;\theta)=(x-y)^2+\theta(y-x)_+.
\]

Stage 3P established a strict global pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium on the open region

\[
r\in\left(\frac{\sqrt{17}-1}{4},1\right),
\qquad
4-2r<\theta<\frac{2r(5r-2)}{2-r},
\]

with

\[
\frac{\partial p_L^*}{\partial r}>0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial p_R^*}{\partial r}<0.
\]

The exact novelty question is therefore:

> Has prior Hotelling/spatial-competition theory already established, or made an immediate corollary, that moving one firm farther away from its rival can simultaneously raise the rival's equilibrium uniform price and lower the moving firm's own equilibrium uniform price because of one-sided directional access/travel friction?

Targeted searches were run through September 2026 using theorem/mechanism language around:

## 1. van der Weijde, Verhoef & van den Berg — asymmetric transport/schedule-delay costs

### Model overlap

This is the strongest architectural predecessor. The model has uniformly distributed consumers, price-sensitive demand, endogenous fares and locations/departure times, and asymmetric early/late schedule-delay costs. It studies the standard location-then-price game, alternative timing, Stackelberg structures and regulation.

### Result comparison

The paper explicitly describes the standard competition-softening effect of increasing separation: when one operator schedules farther from the other, competition is reduced and the operator can increase its fare. The asymmetric-cost extension generates asymmetric fares and locations, but the accessible model/results do not establish the Stage-3P sign pattern in which the moving/right-hand seller's own price falls while its rival's price rises as the same spatial separation increases.

### Classification

`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE / COMPONENT OVERLAP — DOES NOT ABSORB THE FROZEN PROPOSITION`

### Importance for drafting

This paper must be discussed directly. The contrast is useful: a very close asymmetric transport/location/price architecture preserves the usual own-price competition-softening effect of moving farther away, whereas the Stage-3P one-sided soft friction can reverse it for the directionally exposed firm.

## 2. Kharbach (2009) — A Unidirectional Hotelling Model

### Model overlap

Consumers can buy only from firms on one side, creating a hard unidirectional constraint. Firms play a location-price game with quadratic transport costs and uniform pricing.

### Result comparison

For fixed ordered locations, the reported price formulas imply that increasing the right-hand location while holding the left location fixed raises both firms' prices on the relevant ordering. The hard unidirectional benchmark therefore does not generate the Stage-3P opposite-sign price response.

Ebina & Shimizu (2012) later show that Kharbach's claimed location-price outcome is not a subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium. That correction heightens the need for a global equilibrium proof, but it does not supply the Stage-3P price-reversal proposition.

### Classification

`CLOSE BUT DISTINCT — DOES NOT ABSORB`

---

## 3. Colombo (2009, 2011) — unidirectional Hotelling extensions

### Model overlap

The 2009 Economics Bulletin paper extends the hard unidirectional model to spatial price discrimination and general transport costs. The 2011 Journal of Economics paper adds elastic demand under spatial price discrimination.

### Result comparison

The central results concern location under discriminatory pricing, Bertrand/Cournot comparisons, agglomeration/dispersion and collusion. The pricing architecture is materially different from the frozen Stage-3P uniform mill-price theorem. No theorem matching the same-location-change / opposite-price-sign result was identified.

### Classification

`COMPONENT OVERLAP — DIFFERENT PRICING ARCHITECTURE — DOES NOT ABSORB`

---

## 4. Nilssen (1997) and related asymmetric-transport sequential-location work

### Model overlap

Nilssen studies sequential location when movement in one direction is more costly, including entry patterns and first-mover advantage. Related work with Sørgard studies strategic location under asymmetric transport costs with fixed prices.

### Result comparison

These papers strongly absorb any generic claim that directionally asymmetric transport costs alter locations, ordering or mover advantages. They do not establish the frozen uniform-price comparative static because pricing is not the headline endogenous margin in the relevant result.

### Classification

`COMPONENT OVERLAP ON DIRECTIONALITY/LOCATION — DOES NOT ABSORB THE PRICE PROPOSITION`

---

## 5. Cohen & Heifetz (2024) — Fixed Differentiation Principle

### Model overlap

Quadratic Hotelling, covered market, location-then-price competition and an exogenous asymmetry (vertical quality).

### Result comparison

Quality asymmetry polarizes prices: the higher-quality seller raises its price and the lower-quality seller lowers its price. Therefore a broad claim that "asymmetry can make one price rise and the other fall" is already known and is killed.

However, holding the left location fixed and increasing the right location in their price formulas raises both sellers' prices. Their opposite price movements come from changing quality asymmetry, not from one firm's increased horizontal separation.

### Classification

`STRUCTURALLY RELATED — KILLS THE BROAD ASYMMETRIC-PRICE CLAIM, NOT THE FROZEN LOCATION COMPARATIVE STATIC`

---

## 6. Mérel & Sexton (2010) — weak duopoly / perverse price comparative statics

### Model overlap

Hotelling price competition with finite reservation prices, kinked/asymmetric equilibria and a broad analysis of perverse comparative statics.

### Result comparison

They establish that equilibrium price can decrease as transportation cost rises over a nondegenerate weak-duopoly region. Thus the broad statement "greater product differentiation / higher transportation costs can lower equilibrium price" is prior art and is killed.

Their comparative static changes the transportation-cost parameter and is primarily symmetric; it does not establish that moving only the right firm farther from the left firm raises the left price while lowering the right price.

### Classification

`STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE IN RESULT CLASS — KILLS THE BROAD PERVERSE-PRICE CLAIM, NOT THE EXACT PROPOSITION`

---

## 7. Chakravorty & Sappington (2026) — avoidable horizontal differentiation

### Model overlap

Consumers can pay a personal cost to alter a default horizontal characteristic. The resulting switching/default-friction changes sellers' appeal to distant consumers, competitive intensity, equilibrium regimes and profits.

### Result comparison

This is an important recent mechanism threat: a friction that reduces appeal to distant consumers can also commit a seller to compete less vigorously for those consumers. The paper therefore contains a close appeal-versus-competition channel.

However, the parameter being changed is the consumer's avoidable/default-switching friction, not one seller's physical/product location. The accessible results do not state or imply the frozen result that a marginal increase in one seller's location/separation lowers that seller's uniform equilibrium price while raising its rival's price.

### Classification

`STRONG COMPONENT / MECHANISM OVERLAP — DOES NOT ABSORB THE FROZEN LOCATION-PRICE THEOREM`

This is the strongest recent novelty threat and should be cited if the short note proceeds.

## 8. Other recent Hotelling differentiation papers

Recent models with reservation prices, demand/location uncertainty, endogenous/avoidable differentiation and alternative utility specifications reinforce an important discipline point: non-monotone or counterintuitive price effects of differentiation are not novel in general.

They do not, in the reviewed material, provide the exact one-sided directional location comparative static under review.

### Classification

`RELATED BACKGROUND — NO EXACT ABSORPTION FOUND`

# Whole-game/result absorption verdict

No single reviewed predecessor reproduces all of the economically relevant elements of the frozen result:

1. covered Hotelling duopoly with uniform mill prices;
2. symmetric quadratic distance cost plus a one-sided soft directional term;
3. left location fixed, right location varied continuously;
4. strict global pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium;
5. an open parameter region with opposite price responses to the same increase in separation.

The result is nevertheless narrow. It should not be advertised as a new general theory of differentiation, directional competition, or asymmetric pricing.

# Defensible contribution statement

The strongest defensible statement is:

> **One-sided directional access friction can reverse the usual competition-softening price effect of greater horizontal separation for the directionally exposed firm: moving that firm farther from a fixed rival can lower its own equilibrium mill price while raising the rival's price.**

The manuscript should immediately qualify that:

- perverse price effects of differentiation are known in other Hotelling environments;
- asymmetric parameters can generate opposite price changes in other models;
- the contribution is the directional, firm-specific response to the same spatial-separation change under the stated soft-friction structure.

# Evidentiary limit

This is a serious targeted re-kill, not a proof that no unpublished or obscure paper anywhere contains an equivalent theorem. The conclusion is therefore `APPARENTLY OPEN / SURVIVES TARGETED RE-KILL`, not an absolute first-in-literature claim.
