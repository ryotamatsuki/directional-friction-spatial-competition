# Stage 3U — Preferred Minimal Model

Date: 2026-09-04
Status: Stage-3 preferred candidate; **not yet Stage-4 theory freeze**.

## Preferred mechanism

**Demand-responsive directional transit-frequency allocation with a fixed fleet.**

The asymmetry is not imposed as a firm-specific utility wedge. It is generated by background passenger demand in one direction and a third-party transit operator that reallocates scarce service frequency in response to total directional demand, including retail-generated trips.

## Players and primitives

- A unit mass of shoppers is uniformly distributed on `[0,1]`.
- Retailer `L` is at 0 and retailer `R` at 1 in the minimal Stage-4 kill test.
- Retail prices are `p_L,p_R`; common marginal cost is `c`.
- Spatial mismatch/travel disutility is quadratic with coefficient `t>0`.
- A transit operator has total frequency/fleet `F>0` to allocate across left/urban and right/rural directions.
- Waiting/access disutility is `w/f_j`, with `w>0`.
- There is exogenous background urban-bound passenger demand `M>=0`.
- If retailer L's shopping share is `x`, directional transit demand is `M+x` to the left and `1-x` to the right.

## Operator problem

For any shopping share `x`, the operator chooses frequencies to minimize aggregate waiting cost:

\[
\min_{f_L,f_R>0}
 w\left(\frac{M+x}{f_L}+\frac{1-x}{f_R}\right)
\]

subject to

\[
f_L+f_R=F.
\]

The unique interior solution is

\[
f_L(x,M)=F\frac{\sqrt{M+x}}{\sqrt{M+x}+\sqrt{1-x}},
\]

\[
f_R(x,M)=F\frac{\sqrt{1-x}}{\sqrt{M+x}+\sqrt{1-x}}.
\]

This fixed-fleet allocation is the strategic network mediator.

## Shopper equilibrium

Let `x` also denote the marginal shopper and hence L's market share. The shopper indifference condition is

\[
p_L+t x^2+\frac{w}{f_L(x,M)}
=
p_R+t(1-x)^2+\frac{w}{f_R(x,M)}.
\]

Define `A=w/F` and

\[
H(x,M)=\frac{1-M-2x}{\sqrt{(M+x)(1-x)}}.
\]

Then the fixed-point demand equation is

\[
G(x;p_L,p_R,M)
\equiv
p_L-p_R+t(2x-1)+A H(x,M)=0.
\]

Exact derivatives:

\[
H_x=-\frac{(M+1)^2}{2[(M+x)(1-x)]^{3/2}}<0,
\]

\[
H_M=-\frac{M+1}{2\sqrt{1-x}(M+x)^{3/2}}<0.
\]

A locally stable single-valued retail-demand branch requires

\[
g(x,M)\equiv G_x
=2t+A H_x>0.
\]

On such a branch,

\[
\frac{\partial x}{\partial p_L}=-\frac1g,
\qquad
\frac{\partial x}{\partial p_R}=\frac1g.
\]

Because `H_x<0`, endogenous frequency reallocation amplifies price sensitivity relative to fixed frequencies.

## Retail price first-order conditions

Profits are

\[
\pi_L=(p_L-c)x,
\qquad
\pi_R=(p_R-c)(1-x).
\]

On a differentiable stable branch, local price FOCs imply

\[
p_L-c=xg,
\qquad
p_R-c=(1-x)g.
\]

Substitution into shopper equilibrium yields one equilibrium-share equation

\[
K(x,M)
=(2x-1)g+t(2x-1)+A H(x,M)=0.
\]

## Benchmark preflight

At `M=0`, symmetry gives `x=1/2`. Then

\[
g=2t-\frac{4w}{F},
\]

and the symmetric local markup is

\[
p-c=t-\frac{2w}{F}.
\]

Thus the feedback through scarce frequency makes price competition fiercer than standard endpoint quadratic Hotelling, subject to `w/F<t/2` for positive `g`.

Local directional-demand comparative statics at `M=0` are

\[
\left.\frac{dx^*}{dM}\right|_{M=0}
=\frac{w}{3(Ft-2w)}>0,
\]

\[
\left.\frac{d(p_L^*-p_R^*)}{dM}\right|_{M=0}
=\frac{4w}{3F}>0.
\]

## Preferred headline target — Directional Strategic Asymmetry in Prices

This is stronger than the preliminary market-share result.

Let

\[
g'=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}=A H_{xx}.
\]

At a local price equilibrium satisfying the SOCs, the price best-response slopes are

\[
\frac{d BR_L}{dp_R}
=
\frac{g+xg'}{2g+xg'},
\]

\[
\frac{d BR_R}{dp_L}
=
\frac{g-(1-x)g'}{2g-(1-x)g'}.
\]

Moreover,

\[
H_{xx}
=-\frac{3(M+1)^2(M+2x-1)}{4(1-x)^{5/2}(M+x)^{5/2}}.
\]

Hence background directional demand and endogenous frequency reallocation can make the reaction slopes have opposite signs: one retailer treats the rival's price as a strategic substitute while the other continues to treat it as a strategic complement.

### Exact nonempty-region witness

Normalize `t=F=1` and take

\[
M=\frac15,
\qquad
x=\frac{11}{20},
\qquad
\frac{w}{Ft}=\frac{27\sqrt{15}}{308}.
\]

This point satisfies the local equilibrium-share equation. Exact values are

\[
g=\frac{58}{77}>0,
\qquad
g'=-\frac{128}{77}.
\]

For L,

\[
g+xg'=-\frac{62}{385}<0,
\qquad
2g+xg'=\frac{228}{385}>0,
\]

so its price SOC holds and

\[
\frac{dBR_L}{dp_R}=-\frac{31}{114}<0.
\]

For R,

\[
2g-(1-x)g'=\frac{124}{55}>0,
\]

and

\[
\frac{dBR_R}{dp_L}=\frac{289}{434}>0.
\]

Thus the preferred mechanism passes the Stage-3 non-emptiness preflight for **strategic asymmetry**.

## Why the effect is full-architecture candidate

The proposed strategic-asymmetry result disappears in each minimum benchmark:

### B0 — Standard fixed equal frequencies

Frequency does not respond to shopping demand. `g'=0`; both price reaction slopes are `1/2`.

### B1 — Endogenous frequencies but no directional background demand (`M=0`)

At the symmetric equilibrium `x=1/2`, symmetry implies `H_xx=0`, so `g'=0`; both reaction slopes are `1/2`.

### B2 — Directional background demand but shopping demand does not affect frequency

Frequency asymmetry can shift the market intercept, but the access-cost difference is exogenous to `x`; therefore `g'=0` in the standard quadratic demand equation and both reaction slopes remain `1/2`.

### Full architecture

`M>0` plus demand-responsive fixed-fleet frequency gives `g'!=0`; sufficiently strong service-reallocation feedback can create opposite-signed price reaction slopes.

This benchmark map is the main reason to promote the mechanism beyond Stage 3.

## Stage-4 kill contract

Stage 4 may use **only** the ingredients above.

Mandatory tasks:

1. prove existence/uniqueness or characterize multiplicity of the operator-shopper fixed point;
2. derive complete retail price best responses, not only FOCs;
3. establish global price-equilibrium existence and SOC regions;
4. prove an open parameter set supporting opposite-signed price reaction slopes;
5. recover B0/B1/B2 exactly;
6. compare with generic Hotelling network-externality models and strategic-asymmetry theory;
7. determine whether the transport-operator microfoundation adds a theorem or only implements a known reduced-form network effect;
8. derive welfare only after items 1–7 survive.

Not authorized in the initial Stage-4 kill test:

- endogenous retailer locations;
- retailer-funded service;
- government subsidy;
- congestion;
- online/delivery channel;
- additional heterogeneity.
