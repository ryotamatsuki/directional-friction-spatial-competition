\title{Directional Access Friction and the Price Effect of Spatial Separation}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note studies how one-sided directional access friction changes the price effect of spatial separation in a covered Hotelling duopoly. The left firm is fixed at one endpoint and the right firm at an interior location. Consumers face quadratic distance costs plus an additional soft cost when they travel rightward to a destination, and firms set uniform mill prices. The price subgame is solved with global deviations, including deviations that move the marginal consumer across the kink created by the directional term. On a nonempty open parameter region, moving the directionally exposed right firm farther from its fixed rival raises the rival's equilibrium price but lowers the moving firm's own equilibrium price. Greater separation therefore has opposite price effects across firms. The mechanism combines the usual competition-softening effect of spatial separation with increased directional exposure of consumers buying from the moving firm. When the latter effect dominates, its own equilibrium price falls. In the standard quadratic Hotelling benchmark obtained by eliminating the directional component, both equilibrium prices instead rise with separation.
\end{abstract}
