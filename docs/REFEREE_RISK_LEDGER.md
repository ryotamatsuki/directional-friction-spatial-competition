# Referee Risk Ledger — LSRS

## A. “This is just vertical differentiation in disguise.”

**Risk:** high if the directional primitive is described loosely.

**Response in manuscript:** the extra term is `theta(y-x)_+`, whose incidence depends jointly on consumer location and destination. It is not a firm-specific additive utility wedge.

**Status:** addressed in Model section.

## B. “The result is only a local FOC artifact.”

**Risk:** high because the cutoff-price mapping has a kink at the right firm's location.

**Response:** Proposition 1 audits deviations on both sides of the kink, including capture and zero-demand boundaries, and states a strict sufficient global-NE region.

**Status:** addressed.

## C. “Differentiation lowering price is already known.”

**Response:** agree; not the novelty claim. Mérel–Sexton and related work are cited. The paper claims the same one-firm displacement raises the rival's price while lowering the moving firm's own price under one-sided directional exposure.

**Status:** addressed.

## D. “Asymmetry can already move prices in opposite directions.”

**Response:** agree; not the novelty claim. Cohen–Heifetz is cited as a distinct asymmetry comparative static.

**Status:** addressed.

## E. “Why vary `r` if location is not endogenous?”

**Risk:** principal LSRS referee attack.

**Response:** the research design intentionally isolates the global pricing consequence of exogenous spatial separation. The manuscript does not interpret `r` as an equilibrium location choice. Exogenous relocation/product-position changes can be economically meaningful comparative statics even when the location game is outside scope.

**Do not:** reopen Stage 3T or claim location optimality.

## F. “The theorem is functional-form specific.”

**Risk:** real limitation.

**Response:** concede scope. The paper does not claim arbitrary directional-cost robustness. Its contribution is an exact open-region result in a transparent soft-directional Hotelling model.

## G. “One theorem is too thin.”

**Risk:** journal-fit rather than correctness.

**Response:** present a compact Letter package: globally valid equilibrium proposition, opposite-price-response corollary, exact open region, standard quadratic benchmark, and mechanism. Do not inflate with unrelated extensions.

## H. “Global equilibrium uniqueness is overstated.”

**Response:** manuscript states only that the displayed pair is a strict Nash equilibrium and each equilibrium price is the unique global best response to the rival's displayed price. It explicitly does not rule out every other possible equilibrium regime.

## I. “Covered market is unsupported by a valuation bound.”

**Response:** covered unit demand/no outside option is a primitive. A common valuation is normalized out. No outside option is appended after the fact.

## J. “The paper overstates spatial applications.”

**Response:** use only conceptual examples of directional travel/access asymmetry. No empirical claim or policy application is asserted without evidence.
