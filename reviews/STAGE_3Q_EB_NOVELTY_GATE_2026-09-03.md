# Stage 3Q — Economics Bulletin Proposition Novelty Gate

Date: 2026-09-03

## 1. Executive verdict

**GO — EB SHORT-NOTE NOVELTY SURVIVES**

This verdict applies only to the narrow Stage-3P proposition. It does not reopen the upper-field standalone route rejected at Stage 3S.

## 2. Frozen proposition

For the one-sided soft directional friction

\[
\tau(x,y;\theta)=(x-y)^2+\theta(y-x)_+,
\]

with left firm fixed at 0 and right firm at `r`, Stage 3P proves a strict global pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium on an open parameter set for which

\[
\frac{\partial p_L^*}{\partial r}>0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial p_R^*}{\partial r}<0.
\]

Thus the same increase in horizontal separation raises the fixed left rival's price while lowering the moving, directionally exposed right firm's own price.

## 3. Strongest kill attempts

### Generic perverse differentiation-price effect

**Killed as a novelty claim.** Mérel & Sexton (2010) already show that equilibrium price can decrease with transportation cost / degree of differentiation in weak-duopoly Hotelling environments.

### Generic opposite price movements under asymmetry

**Killed as a novelty claim.** Cohen & Heifetz (2024) show quality asymmetry raises one seller's price and lowers the other's.

### Directional/asymmetric transport plus location

**Killed as a broad novelty claim.** Nilssen, Kharbach, Colombo, Ebina–Shimizu and related work establish extensive prior art.

### Exact Stage-3P location-price sign reversal

**Survives.** No reviewed predecessor establishes or immediately implies that moving one firm farther from a fixed rival, under a global uniform-price equilibrium, simultaneously lowers the moving firm's own price and raises the rival's price due to one-sided directional friction.

## 5. Whole-game absorption

No single prior model identified in the targeted audit reproduces the combination of:

- uniform mill prices;
- quadratic distance cost plus one-sided soft directional access cost;
- a continuous movement of only the right firm;
- a strict global pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium;
- opposite equilibrium price derivatives with respect to that movement on an open parameter region.

Therefore the result is not classified as exact prior art or an immediate corollary.

## 6. Permitted novelty language

Permitted:

> One-sided directional access friction can reverse the usual competition-softening price effect of greater horizontal separation for the directionally exposed firm.

Preferred exact statement:

> Moving the directionally exposed firm farther from a fixed rival can lower its own equilibrium mill price while raising its rival's price.

Not permitted:

- `greater differentiation can lower prices`;
- `asymmetry can make one price rise while another falls`;
- `first model with directional transportation costs`;
- `new general principle of spatial differentiation`;
- any claim that the result is proven to be the first in all literature.

## 7. Economics Bulletin floor

Under a concise-note publication standard, the surviving package is potentially sufficient because it contains:

1. a genuine directional primitive rather than the rejected firm-specific wedge;
2. a strict global equilibrium proof;
3. an exact open-region condition;
4. a sharp sign-reversal comparative static;
5. a clean standard-Hotelling nested benchmark at theta=0;
6. a targeted literature distinction from the closest directional and perverse-price predecessors.

## 8. Remaining risks

- A referee may judge the exact comparative static too narrow even for a note.
- An obscure/unindexed predecessor could still contain an equivalent theorem.
- The economic interpretation of `theta(y-x)_+` must be made concrete enough that the result does not look like a manufactured piecewise cost coefficient.
- The paper must keep the global-equilibrium proof concise enough for the target page limit.

These are referee risks, not current hard novelty kills.
