The main new result is not a generic “network effects affect welfare” statement. Because the third-party operator minimizes aggregate waiting cost conditional on the retail market share, the consumer waiting-cost difference is exactly the derivative of real aggregate service cost. ## 2. Frozen model and notation

Retailers L and R are fixed at endpoints 0 and 1. A unit mass of shoppers is uniform on `[0,1]`. If L serves share `x`, directional passenger demands are

\[
D_L=M+x,\qquad D_R=1-x.
\]

Retail prices are `p_L,p_R`, common marginal cost is `c`, quadratic spatial mismatch has coefficient `t`, and the operator has total frequency `F` with minimum service share

\[
q=\underline f/F\in(0,1/2).
\]

Let `s_q(x,M)` denote the left frequency share after clipping the unconstrained square-root allocation to `[q,1-q]`. Then

\[
f_L=Fs_q,\qquad f_R=F(1-s_q).
\]

Let `A=w/F`.

---

## 3. Exact shopper consumer surplus

For gross shopper value `v`, exact shopper surplus is

\[
CS_S
=\int_0^x\left[v-p_L-tz^2-\frac{w}{f_L}\right]dz
+\int_x^1\left[v-p_R-t(1-z)^2-\frac{w}{f_R}\right]dz.
\]

Hence

\[
CS_S
=v-p_Lx-p_R(1-x)
-\frac{t}{3}\{x^3+(1-x)^3\}
-A\left\{\frac{x}{s_q}+\frac{1-x}{1-s_q}\right\}.
\]

This is exact; no triangular-CS shortcut is used.

If background urban-bound passengers have gross value `\bar v` per passenger and only waiting cost is modeled, their surplus contribution is

\[
CS_B=M\bar v-A\frac{M}{s_q}.
\]

Retail producer surplus is

\[
PS=(p_L-c)x+(p_R-c)(1-x).
\]

The model has no variable transit operating-cost function beyond the fixed service resource `F`; any fixed cost of supplying `F` is common across the comparisons below and can be added as a constant.

---

## 4. Total welfare and transfer cancellation

Adding shopper surplus, background-passenger surplus and retail profits cancels retail prices exactly:

\[
TW=v+M\bar v-c-C_q(x),
\]

where real social cost is

\[
C_q(x)=
\frac{t}{3}\{x^3+(1-x)^3\}
+A\left\{\frac{M+x}{s_q(x,M)}+\frac{1-x}{1-s_q(x,M)}\right\}.
\]

Thus all welfare comparisons below are real-cost comparisons; retail markups are transfers.

## 5. Operator envelope identity: transport-specific welfare structure

When the minimum-service floor is slack, the operator solves

\[
\min_{f_L+f_R=F}
w\left(\frac{M+x}{f_L}+\frac{1-x}{f_R}\right).
\]

The minimized waiting cost is

\[
J(x)=A\left(\sqrt{M+x}+\sqrt{1-x}\right)^2.
\]

By direct differentiation / the envelope theorem,

\[
J'(x)
=A\left\{\frac1{s^u(x,M)}-\frac1{1-s^u(x,M)}\right\}
=A H(x,M).
\]

Therefore on the slack branch

\[
C'(x)=t(2x-1)+A H(x,M).
\]

But the shopper indifference condition is exactly

\[
p_L-p_R+C'(x)=0.
\]

This identity is the welfare value added by the transport microfoundation: the same service-allocation response that appears in consumer demand is the marginal derivative of a real aggregate resource/waiting cost. An arbitrary reduced-form network term `Phi(x)` does not by itself identify social welfare this way.

Define

\[
g=C''(x)=2t+A H_x(x,M).
\]

On a stable slack branch, retail FOCs imply

\[
p_L-c=xg,\qquad p_R-c=(1-x)g.
\]

Hence at an interior retail price equilibrium,

\[
C'(x^{NE})=-(2x^{NE}-1)g(x^{NE}).
\]

### Welfare wedge proposition

If `g>0`:

- when `x^{NE}>1/2`, `C'(x^{NE})<0`: moving additional shoppers toward L raises welfare locally;
- when `x^{NE}<1/2`, `C'(x^{NE})>0`: moving additional shoppers toward R raises welfare locally;
- only at `x^{NE}=1/2` does this local share wedge vanish.

Thus decentralized price competition **dampens the socially valuable concentration toward the already larger-demand direction** generated by the service-frequency scale economy. This is the welfare interpretation of the same fixed-fleet feedback that underlies S1.

The sign identity is general for the operator-mediated class, but the broad idea that network externalities create private/social adoption wedges is not claimed as new.

## 6. Exact witness: private versus second-best allocation

Use the hardened exact witness

\[
t=F=1,\quad c=0,\quad M=\frac23,\quad q=\frac13,
\]

\[
x^{NE}=\frac{23}{40}=0.575,
\qquad
A=\frac{22797\sqrt{7599}}{7364680}\approx0.269837336.
\]

At the retail equilibrium,

\[
g=\frac{188234}{184117},
\]

so

\[
C'(x^{NE})
=-\frac{282351}{1841170}
\approx-0.153354<0.
\]

The decentralized equilibrium therefore has too little urban-side concentration relative to the social optimum.

At `q=1/3`, the rural/right service floor binds for `x>=2/3`. In that region,

\[
C_q(x)=
\frac13\{x^3+(1-x)^3\}
+A\left\{\frac{M+x}{2/3}+\frac{1-x}{1/3}\right\}.
\]

Its unique minimizer is

\[
x^{SB}(q)
=\frac12+\frac{A(1-2q)}{2tq(1-q)}.
\]

For `q=1/3`,

\[
x^{SB}
=\frac12+\frac{68391\sqrt{7599}}{29458720}
\approx0.702378.
\]

This lies strictly inside the floor-binding region and is the global second-best allocation subject to the service obligation.

Real social cost is

\[
C_q(x^{NE})\approx0.930726,
\qquad
C_q(x^{SB})\approx0.919348.
\]

Thus the second-best welfare gain relative to the decentralized equilibrium is

\[
C_q(x^{NE})-C_q(x^{SB})\approx0.011378>0.
\]

The gap is about 1.22% of decentralized real cost under the witness normalization.

### First-best without the service obligation

If the minimum-service constraint is removed from the planner problem, exact/numerical root isolation for the witness shows the unconstrained real-cost function is decreasing up to the right endpoint. The utilitarian first-best therefore sets `x^{FB}=1` and shuts down right-direction service.

This is **not** a policy recommendation. It shows that the minimum-service obligation represents a service-availability / coverage objective not valued separately in the current utilitarian welfare function. Stage 7 therefore treats `x^{SB}(q)`—not the no-floor corner—as the policy-relevant social benchmark once the service obligation is institutionally imposed.

## 7. A nonbinding regulatory band

For the exact witness, the equilibrium frequency share is approximately

\[
s^*=0.6308955,
\]

so the right service floor is slack at equilibrium whenever

\[
q<1-s^*\approx0.369105.
\]

Because the equilibrium is unchanged while the floor is slack, prices, shares, frequencies and on-path welfare are identical across all floor levels that support the same equilibrium.

However, the floor changes off-equilibrium deviations. Analytic partition of the unilateral deviation problems plus one-dimensional root isolation gives two thresholds for the exact witness:

\[
q_L\approx0.324091,
\qquad
q_U\approx0.344228.
\]

For the strict interval

\[
q\in(q_L,q_U),
\]

the same strategic-asymmetry candidate is a strict global price Nash equilibrium and the floor remains slack on path.

- If `q<q_L`, L can profitably cut price enough to push demand toward the high-L boundary before the service floor activates.
- If `q>q_U` (while still slack at the candidate), the floor protects R strongly enough after large deviations to create a profitable R deviation.

This yields an **intermediate implementation band**: a nonbinding service standard can be too weak or too strong off path even though it has zero direct effect on the equilibrium allocation.

This is retained as a Stage-7 policy/implementation result, not as a claim that the regulator optimally chooses `q` in the current model.

## 8. Functional-form generality

Replace waiting disutility `w/f` by

\[
a(f)=w f^{-\rho},\qquad \rho>0.
\]

Conditional on directional demands `D_L,D_R`, the unconstrained fixed-fleet optimum is

\[
f_i
=F\frac{D_i^{1/(\rho+1)}}{D_L^{1/(\rho+1)}+D_R^{1/(\rho+1)}}.
\]

The minimized waiting cost is

\[
J_\rho(D_L,D_R)
=\frac{w}{F^\rho}
\left(D_L^{1/(\rho+1)}+D_R^{1/(\rho+1)}\right)^{\rho+1}.
\]

The envelope identity survives:

\[
\frac{dJ_\rho}{dx}
=a(f_L^*)-a(f_R^*).
\]

Hence the welfare-demand identity and the mechanism are not artifacts of the square-root case `rho=1`.

A deterministic continuation/global-deviation audit holding the witness's `M,q,A` fixed finds a neighborhood around `rho=1` in which the global equilibrium and opposite-signed reaction slopes survive; a conservative verified range is approximately

\[
0.95\le\rho\le1.01.
\]

The exact endpoints are not claimed as theorem thresholds. The theoretical claim is local robustness by continuity because all Stage-5 equilibrium and deviation inequalities are strict at `rho=1`.

## 9. Generality across institutions

### Setting A — directional fixed-route / shuttle service with deadheading or short-turn operations

Directional passenger imbalance can make operators deadhead vehicles or concentrate effective frequency in the peak direction. With a fixed vehicle pool, this maps directly into the shared-resource reallocation mechanism. Minimum headways provide the service-floor analogue.

Applicability is strongest when a commercial destination or cluster is large enough that destination demand matters for service planning. A single ordinary retailer is less plausible.

### Setting B — demand-responsive transit / shared mobility fleet allocation across zones

A fixed DRT fleet can be dynamically allocated/repositioned toward zones with more requests. Background commuting creates asymmetric baseline demand, while destination prices can affect discretionary trip requests. Minimum zonal service or response-time requirements play the role of the service floor.

This setting avoids the strongest physical objection to treating opposite directions of a conventional two-way route as independently reallocable frequencies.

The mechanism does not require adding a new strategic margin in either setting.

## 10. Empirical predictions

1. **Demand-mediated service response:** a destination-side retail demand shock should change service allocation toward that direction/zone when the operator is demand-responsive, but not under a fixed schedule.
2. **Cross-rival access effect:** service gained by one direction should be accompanied by service loss or longer waits in the other direction when total fleet is fixed.
3. **Strategic-response asymmetry:** under sufficiently strong directional background demand, the high-background-demand retailer/cluster can respond to a rival price increase by lowering rather than raising its own price.
4. **Service-floor kink:** once a minimum service floor binds, further demand shifts cease to withdraw frequency from the protected direction, reducing the amplification of retail demand responses.
5. **Fleet-size attenuation:** larger `F` lowers `A=w/F`, weakening the network-mediated pricing effect and moving the game toward standard Hotelling complementarity.
6. **Background-demand amplification:** larger directional background demand shifts retail share and relative price toward the high-background-demand side near the symmetric benchmark.

## 11. Policy scope and limits

Modeled policy instrument: the minimum service share `q` only.

Permitted conclusion:

> A minimum-service obligation may support a downstream price equilibrium by truncating extreme off-equilibrium service reallocation while remaining nonbinding in normal equilibrium operation.

Not permitted from the current model:

- claims about optimal subsidies;
- transit investment / fleet expansion policy;
- endogenous retailer location;
- optimal land-use policy;
- equity benefits of minimum service (equity is not in the welfare function);
- claims specific to Umeda–Minami-Kusatsu without separate evidence.

## 12. Main concerns after Stage 7

1. **Institutional scale:** whether a retailer or commercial cluster is large enough to move transit frequency is context-dependent and not established for the motivating example.
2. **Two-way vehicle conservation:** on a conventional fixed route, directional frequencies are linked by vehicle circulation. The model is most defensible for deadheading/short-turn operations, separate service pools, shuttles, or DRT/repositioning rather than a frictionless long-run two-way timetable.
3. **Reduced-form absorption remains:** the local pricing result can still be written using a generic market-share externality. The strongest defense is now the operator-envelope welfare identity, the off-path service-floor implementation result, and the institutional mapping.
4. **Service-floor welfare objective:** the floor is institutionally real but its equity/coverage rationale is outside the utilitarian welfare function. It should be treated as an exogenous service obligation, not an efficiency instrument.

None is a fatal mathematical contradiction. They determine positioning and exposition.
