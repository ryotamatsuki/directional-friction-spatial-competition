## Strongest prior-art attacks

### Tombak (2006)

Directly absorbs the term and concept `strategic asymmetry`: one firm may view a rival's strategy as a complement while the rival views the first firm's strategy as a substitute. The paper also discusses price-competition examples. The present paper must never market the sign pattern itself as a new game-theory concept.

### Grilo, Shy & Thisse (2001)

Combines spatial duopoly with consumption/network externalities. Weak conformity intensifies price competition; strong conformity creates multiplicity/tipping; the paper also examines conditions for more general increasing concave externality functions. This is the strongest threat to reduced-form novelty.

### Griva & Vettas (2011)

Differentiated duopoly with product-specific network effects and price-influenced expectations. Price competition can intensify, market shares become asymmetric, and strong effects produce equilibrium multiplicity. Again, a generic network-feedback pricing claim is absorbed.

### Tolotti & Yepez (2020)

Direct Hotelling-Bertrand model with firm-specific network effects and full characterization of location, price and demand equilibria. This is the closest mature Hotelling-network family and forces the contribution to rest on the third-party shared-fleet strategic architecture rather than a reduced-form clientele effect.

### Bar-Yosef, Martens & Benenson (2013)

Directly models demand-frequency vicious/virtuous cycles and multiple equilibria in bus service. P1 therefore has no independent novelty value.

### Hotelling network effects plus stabilizing friction

Prior work shows that switching costs can restore pure price equilibrium otherwise destabilized by network externalities. Therefore 'a regularizer restores equilibrium' is not a new theorem category. The surviving distinction is that the current minimum service floor is an upstream operator constraint that is slack at equilibrium and changes only sufficiently large downstream deviations.

### Toshimitsu (2026)

A current Japanese Economic Review paper adds network connectivity to Hotelling and studies Bertrand/Cournot profit rankings, endogenous strategic-variable choice and welfare. This is a recent broad threat but does not reproduce the third-party demand-responsive fixed-fleet mechanism or the surviving P2/P3 result.

## Whole-game absorption test

No single inspected predecessor reproduces all of the following:

1. two downstream spatial retailers choosing prices;
2. a third-party operator controlling a shared fixed transit resource;
3. service allocation responding to retailer-generated directional passenger demand;
4. exogenous directional background demand;
5. retail firms anticipating the induced reallocation of access quality across both directions;
6. a service floor slack at equilibrium but truncating extreme continuation responses;
7. a global price equilibrium on an open set with opposite-signed retailer price reaction slopes.

The full strategic feedback is therefore not treated as absorbed merely because each ingredient separately exists in prior work.

## Surviving contribution

### S1 — Network-mediated downstream strategic asymmetry

The frozen hardened model admits a nonempty open set of global pure price equilibria with `BR_L' < 0 < BR_R'`, generated by the interaction of directional background demand and retail-responsive shared-fleet reallocation.

### S2 — Off-equilibrium support by a slack service obligation

A minimum service obligation can be strictly slack at the S1 equilibrium but necessary for global support because it prevents only sufficiently extreme deviations from triggering service collapse.

### S3 — Nested interaction

S1 disappears under each minimum benchmark: fixed frequency, no directional background demand, or retail-unresponsive frequency.
