## Why this is a restart rather than a revision

The rejected Economics Bulletin model used a destination-specific additive wedge on purchases from one firm. External review correctly identified that the wedge did not vary with the direction of movement. The old core primitive therefore cannot be retained while continuing to claim a directional-transport contribution.

This repository reopens theory at Stage 3. The old paper is evidence and provenance only; it is not a theory freeze for the new project.

## Candidate core primitive

For consumer location `x` and firm location `y`, define

\[
\tau(x,y;\theta)=(x-y)^2+\theta (y-x)_+,
\qquad (z)_+=\max\{z,0\},\quad \theta\ge 0.
\]

The additional friction is tied to rightward movement rather than firm identity.

## Core research question

Can one-sided directional transport friction change the existence set of pure-strategy price equilibria, and can that existence frontier endogenously discipline sequential location choices?

## Contribution routes to test, not presume

1. **Price-equilibrium existence frontier**: characterize
   \[
   \mathcal E(\theta)=\{(l,r):0\le l<r\le1,\ \text{the price subgame has a pure-strategy Nash equilibrium}\}.
   \]
2. **Directional comparative statics**: determine how `E(theta)` moves as `theta` changes and whether the frontier is genuinely asymmetric.
3. **Endogenous location principle**: test whether leader/follower choices are pinned by, or systematically approach, the boundary of `E(theta)`.
4. **New location regime**: test whether the resulting equilibrium differs economically from maximum differentiation, fixed differentiation, and existing asymmetric/directional sequential-location results.

No claim survives until it passes Stage 6 novelty re-kill.

## Hard kill order

### Kill Test 0 — Global price-equilibrium existence

For arbitrary `0 <= l < r <= 1` and `theta >= 0`:

- derive piecewise demand for all possible indifferent-consumer regions;
- solve candidate price FOCs within each region;
- check all cross-regime unilateral price deviations;
- characterize existence/nonexistence of a global pure-strategy price Nash equilibrium;
- treat corners, zero demand, monopoly capture, and boundary indifference explicitly.

A regime-local FOC is not accepted as a Nash equilibrium proof.

### Kill Test 1 — Nested benchmark

At `theta=0`, recover the standard quadratic-transport benchmark exactly. Any discontinuity or contradiction must be resolved before proceeding.

### Kill Test 2 — Directional existence comparative statics

Characterize whether `E(theta)` contracts, expands, or deforms non-monotonically as `theta` rises. Mere numerical plots do not count as proof.

### Kill Test 3 — Sequential location

Only after Kill Tests 0–2 pass:

- leader chooses `a`;
- follower chooses `b`;
- firms price simultaneously;
- solve the follower's global location problem, including possible leapfrogging and nonexistence regions;
- solve the leader problem anticipating the follower's response.

Primary question: does endogenous location respond to the price-equilibrium existence frontier in a way not generated by standard differentiation incentives alone?

### Kill Test 4 — Exact prior-art re-kill

Mandatory closest-paper families:

- maximum-differentiation / standard quadratic Hotelling;
- horizontal × vertical differentiation and fixed-differentiation results;
- Nilssen-style asymmetric transportation costs and sequential location;
- hard directional/unidirectional Hotelling models;
- price-equilibrium existence under nonlinear or linear-quadratic transport costs;
- spatial models where price-equilibrium existence itself constrains locations.

## Stop conditions

Return `NO-GO` if any of the following holds:

- the price game lacks a useful pure-strategy equilibrium on economically relevant location regions and no new theorem emerges from that fact;
- the existence frontier is an immediate corollary of known nonlinear-transport existence results;
- the sequential-location result is equivalent to an existing asymmetric-location result after parameter mapping;
- the only novelty is the exact functional form `theta (y-x)_+`;
- the only new result is a numerical regime diagram without an analytical mechanism.

## Deferred items

Do **not** add an optimal-policy stage yet. Friction-reduction policy is admissible only after a new equilibrium/location result survives Stage 6 and the welfare channel is non-mechanical.
